# Explore and final modes, with a test of how much they differ

Every inversion config has a `mode`: `explore` sets fast defaults (Cholesky at a few damping values or a coarse damping search, GPU float32 for columns and products, NSIDE 32 cells summed from NSIDE 64, short sampler chains, uncertainties only where cheap) for scanning many settings quickly; `final` sets robust defaults (full eigendecomposition with the evidence curve and posterior uncertainties, float64, full resolution, long chains with convergence checks) for answers that are reported. Each default can be overridden on its own; every result records its mode and settings, figures and the viewer mark explore results, and a final rerun of the same config reuses whatever is cached at final quality. We do not pick one speed-robustness trade-off for the user, because both needs are real: fast searches over settings, and the most robust answer for a reported result.

So that explore results can be trusted for searching, each method has a mode-agreement test, part of its stage's acceptance: the same configs (synthetic and real) run in both modes, with the differences reported against the final result's own uncertainty: VIS difference in posterior standard deviations, damping and evidence, and for domain methods the agreement of the partitions (adjusted Rand index) and of the number of domains.

## Amendment (2026-10-08): domain methods keep their cells in explore mode

Coarser cells were the explore default for every method, but the mode-agreement tests of stages 2 and 3 showed that for domain methods they change the question rather than blur the answer. PD's domains are assigned whole coarse cells, so small domains lose much of their area (one vanished) and values moved by up to 6.5 noise standard deviations; FD-merge can only draw boundaries in 200 km steps, its calibrated merge penalty fell from 1304 to 806, and its partitions barely matched final (adjusted Rand index 0.1-0.6). Domain methods (PD, FD-merge, and FD-vor, FD-lvor and FD-part when they come) therefore keep the 100 km cells in explore mode and save time with fewer repeat runs: PD 30 noise draws instead of 100, FD-merge 10 null and 10 bootstrap runs instead of 20 and 30. Explore and final then answer the same question, with rougher error estimates in explore. Cell maps, regressions and bounds keep coarser cells in explore, where they only blur the map.
