# DF: independent fractal patterns per domain, measured noise only, a shared exponent

DF models VIS as a mean per domain plus a Gaussian random pattern inside each domain, with a power-law spectrum (the fractal form of the magnetisation literature). The patterns of different domains are independent: they start afresh at domain boundaries, because the boundaries stand for geological breaks, and the evidence of a layout then tests whether those breaks are real. Each domain has its own mean (uniform on 0-4 SI·km) and strength; the fractal exponent β and the largest patch size are shared by all domains, because inside one domain the data (features of about 300-2500 km) span too few sizes to fix an exponent. β is fitted within the published range (2-3 for vertically integrated maps: 3-4 for the 3-D magnetisation, Pilkington & Todoeschuck 1993, Maus & Dimri 1995, less one for the depth sum).

The data error is the measurement noise only, without the model-error term that CM's error model takes from the field's own spectrum: that term stands in for variation the model leaves out, which DF now models, and keeping both would count it twice and shrink the strengths.

The pattern is Gaussian, so the evidence is exact and fast, and VIS can dip below zero inside a domain; how much does so is reported, with a bounded map where it matters, rather than using a log-normal pattern (always positive, but no exact evidence). Strengths, β and the patch size maximise the evidence, with error bars from its curvature; sampling them (MCMC) is planned as v2.

Considered: one pattern over the whole area whose statistics change between domains (almost the per-class GMRF of CM and CR; softer boundaries, no test of the breaks); strength and exponent per domain (exponents undetermined); the CM error model (double counting).

## Amendment (2026-10-09): an extra error for the ring of data around the target

On the Australian data the noise-only error left a misfit of 52 nT in the ring of data points around the target (up to 300 km beyond it), where no domain can explain the field: errors of the fixed background or sources just outside. Fitting one noise scale everywhere absorbed it but inflated the errors onshore too (scale 2.5) and pushed beta from one end of its range to the other. The data in the ring therefore get an extra error of their own (correlated like the field model, its RMS fitted by the evidence: 62 nT), which fits better than the inflated noise and leaves the onshore fit unchanged; a noise scale fitted on top now gains little. The onshore data keep the noise-only error of the decision above.

## Amendment (2026-10-10): domain means drawn from a shared distribution

With the means uniform on 0-4 SI·km and integrated over all values, the continents' DF fits left 15-16% of cells below zero in Africa and South America, from domain means below zero (16 African domains, 4 South American). A cap of 2500 km on the patch size changed nothing. The data see a large domain's level mainly at its edges (a uniform +0.5 SI·km over Southern Africa is cancelled to 95% by the cells within 400 km of its edge), so the prior has to carry it, and a flat prior lets it run off. The means can therefore be drawn instead from N(v0, tau^2), with v0 uniform on the box and tau fitted by the evidence (`[method] means = "gaussian"`), as GCM's domain levels are (ADR 0008). Integrating out the offsets adds tau^2 U U' to the data covariance, so tau is fitted, given error bars and sampled like the ring error. Holding the means inside the box was the alternative; it only clips them, and a poorly seen level would then sit at a bound. On Zealandia (GCM-GLC background), the Gaussian means raise the evidence of the polygons by 30 over the box, and the patch size leaves its upper bound (5000 to 315 km).
